## Abstract

Planning a Tagaytay trip as a group usually means a chat thread, a map app and a few review sites. Public data shows how much of that work falls on travelers. In 2022, the PSA found that 97.9% of Filipino domestic travelers planned their own trips. Tagaytay drew more than 6.5 million same-day visitors in the last quarter of 2023 alone. For our capstone thesis, I led development of TravelEase, a tourism portal for Tagaytay. A group plans one trip together, finds local businesses on an interactive map, and reads reviews and blogs. Business listings go live only after local government approval. TravelEase has five user roles and 411 automated tests, and it is live at travelease.app.

## The problem I saw

Travelers heading to Tagaytay need to plan together, find local businesses and know what to expect on the road. Local businesses need a trusted place to be listed, and the local government needs a say in which registrations go live.

We wanted a capstone that real people could use, not just a demo for the panel. Tagaytay is a popular weekend trip from Manila. Planning one as a group usually means a chat thread, a map app and a few review sites. We built one place for all of it.

Each of those tools holds one part of the trip. The chat holds the plan, the map holds the route, and the review sites hold what to expect. None of them knows about the others, so the group has to stitch the trip together by hand.

Businesses face the other side of the same gap. A listing only helps if travelers can trust it, and a check by the local government gives them a reason to.

## What the data says

Public data shows that domestic travel is large, that Tagaytay is one of its busiest stops, and that the planning falls on travelers themselves.

| Measure | Figure | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Filipinos aged 15 and over who traveled in the country, January to September 2022 | 39.1% (30.89 million travelers) | PSA |
| Domestic travelers who planned their own trips, 2022 | 97.9% (2.1% took package tours) | PSA |
| Domestic tourism expenditure, 2025 | ₱3.26 trillion, up 3% from 2024 | PSA, via Manila Bulletin |
| Overnight travelers in CALABARZON, 2024 | 10,863,098, of whom 10,680,294 were local | DOT, via Manila Bulletin |
| Same-day visitors to Tagaytay, October to December 2023 | More than 6.5 million | Cavite provincial tourism office, via the Inquirer |

**Most Filipinos who travel plan the trip themselves.** In the [PSA's 2022 Household Survey on Domestic Visitors](https://psa.gov.ph/system/files/sdsd/2022%20HSDV%20Press%20Release_signed.pdf), 39.1% of Filipinos 15 and over traveled within the country from January to September 2022. That was 30.89 million domestic travelers. Pleasure or vacation was the top reason for travel, at 39.7%. And 97.9% of domestic travelers planned their own trips, while only 2.1% chose a package tour.

**Domestic tourism is large and still growing.** The PSA's tourism accounts, [reported by Manila Bulletin](https://mb.com.ph/2026/05/28/tourism-contributed-81-to-philippine-economy-in-2025psa), put domestic tourism spending at ₱3.26 trillion in 2025. That was up 3% from ₱3.16 trillion in 2024.

**CALABARZON is the most visited region.** The Department of Tourism counted 10,863,098 overnight travelers in CALABARZON in 2024, [Manila Bulletin reported](https://mb.com.ph/2026/02/28/calabarzon-continues-reign-as-most-visited-region-since-2008). Of those, 10,680,294 were local tourists. It was the country's most visited region for 16 straight years. Tagaytay is in Cavite, one of the region's provinces.

**Tagaytay is mostly a day trip.** Tagaytay had more than 6.5 million same-day visitors from October to December 2023, according to [the Inquirer's report on Cavite's tourism figures](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1936826/tagaytay-is-most-visited-tourist-spot-in-cavite-in-q4-2023). Around 268,000 stayed overnight in the same period. The data came from the Cavite provincial tourism office, and it made Tagaytay the most visited place in Cavite that quarter.

**Group planning is hard to keep in one place.** In a [Samsung-commissioned survey of UK travelers](https://news.samsung.com/uk/unsung-holiday-heroes-new-research-spotlights-the-person-who-turns-group-holiday-ideas-into-reality) from July 2026, 46% struggle to keep track of the group chat, bookings and everyone's preferences. Finding dates that work for everyone was the top challenge, at 42%. And 64% said a group getaway would never get off the ground without one organizer. The survey covers UK travelers and does not publish its sample size, so it shows the pattern, not Philippine numbers.

**Tourism listings already need a local check.** Under the Tourism Act of 2009 rules, local governments may permit primary tourism enterprises to operate only after DOT accreditation. The [DILG reminded local governments of this rule](https://calabarzon.dilg.gov.ph/dilg-to-lgus-issue-permit-to-operate-to-tourism-enterprises-with-dot-accreditation-only/) in January 2024. Those enterprises include hotels, resorts, homestays and tour operators. DILG Secretary Benjamin Abalos Jr. said accreditation protects tourists from "unreliable transactions from sketchy service providers." The [DOT's accreditation page](https://tourism.gov.ph/accreditations/online) describes accreditation as official recognition that an enterprise meets minimum standards.

## Why I chose this solution

Each part of TravelEase lines up with one of those findings. The table maps what a group needs to the tool it usually uses, and to the part of TravelEase that covers it.

| What the group needs | Usual tool | In TravelEase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Agree on one plan | A chat thread | A shared travel plan with admin, editor and viewer roles |
| Find places and get there | A map app | A business directory on an interactive map, with routing, traffic and weather |
| Know what to expect | A few review sites | Reviews and travel blogs for each place |
| Trust a listing | Not covered by the usual three | Local government approval of business registrations |

**One shared plan, with roles.** Nearly all domestic travelers plan their own trips, so the planning sits with the group, not a tour operator. The UK survey shows where that work breaks down: keeping the chat, bookings and preferences together. A shared plan puts the trip in one place. The admin role fits the single organizer that 64% of UK respondents said a group trip depends on. Editors help shape the plan, and viewers follow along.

**One place instead of three.** Travelers in other markets show the same pull toward one platform. [Expedia Group's 2026 research with Harris Poll](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260602270896/en/) surveyed more than 2,500 travelers. Of them, 77% were at least somewhat likely to book more than one part of their next trip on the same platform. The survey covered ten countries, and the Philippines was not one of them.

**A map built for the road.** Travelers need to know what to expect on the road, so the map carries routing, traffic and weather, not just pins. The Tagaytay numbers support that focus. Same-day visitors outnumbered overnight guests by about 24 to one in late 2023 (estimate: 6.5 million ÷ 268,000). On a day trip, time on the road shapes the whole plan.

**Reviews and blogs beside each place.** Pleasure or vacation was the top reason Filipinos traveled in 2022. A leisure trip depends on knowing what a place is like before going. Putting reviews and travel blogs on each place's page means the group reads them where it plans.

**Local government approval for listings.** Local governments already decide which tourism businesses may operate. For primary tourism enterprises, their permits depend on DOT accreditation. Making a registration wait for local government approval puts that existing role in front of travelers. It also gives the local government the say over listings that the problem called for.

**Both sides, not one.** A directory alone would help businesses but leave the group in its chat thread. A planner alone would leave travelers without trusted listings. The problem had both sides, so TravelEase covers both, across five user roles for travelers, businesses and administrators.

## What I built

- **Collaborative travel plans** with admin, editor and viewer roles, so a group can plan one trip together.
- **A business directory** in 8 categories, with local government approval of business registrations.
- **An interactive map** with routing, plus traffic and weather information.
- **Reviews and travel blogs** for each place.
- **Five user roles** across travelers, businesses and administrators.

## How it works

TravelEase connects three kinds of users, travelers, businesses and administrators, across five user roles.

- **Planning:** a travel plan belongs to a group. Admin, editor and viewer roles decide who manages the plan, who changes it and who only reads it, so everyone works from the same plan.
- **Listing:** a business registers in one of 8 categories. The registration waits for local government approval, and only approved businesses go live in the directory.
- **Finding:** travelers find local businesses on the interactive map, built with Leaflet, and get routing plus traffic and weather information.
- **Deciding:** each place has its own reviews and travel blogs.
- **Staying fast:** Redis caches busy pages and rate-limits requests.

## How I built and tested it

TravelEase was our capstone thesis, built from October 2025 to April 2026. I was the development lead. The project has 316 commits, and about two-thirds of them are mine. It has 411 automated tests in Vitest.

- **Front end:** React 19, TypeScript, Vite, TanStack Query, Tailwind CSS 4 and Leaflet for the map.
- **Back end:** Node.js and Express, with Prisma on PostgreSQL and Supabase Auth for sign-in.
- **Performance and safety:** Redis for caching and rate limiting.
- **Testing:** Vitest.
- **Hosting:** Vercel and Railway.

React with TypeScript and Vite kept the front end quick to build and safe to change as a team. Express with Prisma on PostgreSQL fit the many links between trips, businesses, reviews and roles. Supabase Auth saved us from building sign-in. Redis kept busy pages responsive, and Vercel with Railway kept the site live after the defense.

The stack still needs care after the defense. The [Prisma ORM 8 release candidate](/blog/prisma-orm-8-release-candidate) touches this project, and so did the [October 2026 Railway security incident](/blog/railway-security-incident-environment-variables), since TravelEase is hosted on Railway. TravelEase is also built on React 19, the line covered in my post on [React 19.3 View Transitions](/blog/react-19-3-view-transitions).

## Limitations

- **Usage is not recorded here.** This page does not record how many travelers signed up, how many trips groups planned, or how many businesses registered. It shows what TravelEase does, not how many people used it.
- **The approval flow is described, not measured.** This page does not record which local government office reviews registrations, or how long approval takes.
- **Approval is not accreditation.** TravelEase's approval is a local step on the platform. It is not DOT accreditation, which covers primary tourism enterprises such as hotels, resorts and tour operators.
- **The data has gaps.** The PSA's latest published domestic visitor survey covers 2022. The Tagaytay figures cover one quarter, from a provincial office through a news report. No Philippine study of group trip planning across apps could be verified, so that point rests on UK and ten-country surveys.
- **What would make it stronger:** four numbers. How many apps users juggled before, how many plans had more than one member, approval times, and use of traffic and weather data.
