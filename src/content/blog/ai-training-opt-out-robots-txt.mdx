A single robots.txt rule, `User-Agent: __usage_ai-training` followed by `Disallow: /`, will soon tell Google, Microsoft Bing and Anthropic not to use a site's content for AI training, without changing how the site ranks in regular search.

This take is part of my [SEO, GEO and AEO](/topics/seo-geo-aeo) writing.

## What happened?

On October 30, 2026, Microsoft Bing published [Your content, your choice: clearer preferences for training and grounding](https://blogs.bing.com/search/2026/10/your-content-your-choice-clearer-preferences-for-training-and-grounding/). It splits AI use into two preferences: **AI training**, a shared industry signal set in robots.txt, and **AI grounding**, Bing's term for using a page to inform AI answers, set page by page.

The same day, Kevin Kelley of Anthropic posted [the training preference signal draft](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-kelley-aipref-training-preference-signal/) to the IETF. His [message to the AI Preferences working group](https://mailarchive.ietf.org/arch/msg/ai-control/mddjatOxSR0h6GmLRBWlc839c7w/) says Anthropic has started implementing it, with rollout across its services taking time. It is an individual draft, not yet an IETF standard.

Google's [common crawlers documentation](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/crawlers-fetchers/google-common-crawlers) now says Google "intends to support the AIPREF shared machine-readable training preference signal" in early 2027. According to [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-ai-training-control-42430.html), Google's John Mueller said a site that opts out with the token, without naming Google-Extended, will be treated as disallowing Google-Extended. The same report says OpenAI made a similar move.

## What changed?

The opt-out is an ordinary robots.txt group:

```txt
User-Agent: __usage_ai-training
Disallow: /
```

- **Existing files say nothing.** The draft says `User-Agent: *` groups never match the token, so a robots.txt without this group expresses no preference.
- **Paths work.** You can disallow one folder, or allow one back, using normal robots.txt path rules.
- **Bing adds page-level controls.** A `bingbot` meta tag or `X-Robots-Tag` header can set `ai-training:disallow`, and grounding can be set to `ai-grounding:disallow` or `ai-grounding:disallowextended`. There is no site-wide grounding setting in robots.txt.
- **Both default to Allow** when nothing is published.

Bing's timeline:

| Date | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| December 15, 2026 | Temporary grounding preferences open in Bing Webmaster Tools |
| February 1, 2027 | The new preferences take effect as pages are crawled |
| March 1, 2027 | NoArchive and NoCache stop counting as training or grounding preferences |
| September 30, 2027 | Temporary grounding preferences expire |

## What does it mean for businesses and site owners?

Training and AI answers are now separate choices, so a small business can say no to training and still be cited. Bing says Disallow or DisallowExtended on grounding will reduce brand visibility in AI experiences, and Google says [Google-Extended](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/crawlers-fetchers/google-common-crawlers) does not affect inclusion in Google Search.

One catch, as I read Google's docs: Google-Extended also covers grounding in Gemini, and Mueller said sites that explicitly allow Google-Extended stay eligible there. If you block training with the shared token but still want Gemini to cite you, allow Google-Extended explicitly. Google's separate opt-out for AI Overviews and AI Mode is the [Search Console control I covered earlier](/news/google-search-generative-ai-control).

## What should you do now?

1. **Decide on training separately** from showing up in AI answers.
2. **Do nothing** if you are happy to allow training, the default.
3. **Add the group** above if you want to opt out.
4. **Check NoArchive and NoCache** if you use them, and set Bing's new preferences before March 1, 2027.
