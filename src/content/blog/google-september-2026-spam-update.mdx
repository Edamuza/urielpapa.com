Google released the September 2026 spam update on September 24, 2026, and finished rolling it out on October 8, 2026. It is Google's fourth spam update this year and by far the longest. If your Google traffic moved in late September or early October, this is the first thing to rule in or out.

This take is part of my [SEO, GEO and AEO](/topics/seo-geo-aeo) writing. Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode draw on the same Search index, as I explain in [GEO and AEO fundamentals](/blog/geo-and-aeo-fundamentals). A page that drops for spam also loses its chance to be cited there.

## What happened?

Google posted the update on its [Search Status Dashboard](https://status.search.google.com/incidents/XhUDXP7A67iHCD2kmbVu) at 09:15 Pacific time on September 24, 2026. The entry says the update applies globally and to all languages, and that the rollout could take up to two weeks. On October 8, Google added that the rollout was complete as of that day.

Google did not say which kind of spam the update targets. The [dashboard history](https://status.search.google.com/products/rGHU1u87FJnkP6W2GwMi/history) shows how it compares with the other 2026 spam updates:

| Update | Started | Duration on the dashboard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| March 2026 spam update | March 24 | 19 hours, 30 minutes |
| June 2026 spam update | June 24 | 2 days, 1 hour |
| August 2026 spam update | August 18 | 2 days, 16 hours |
| September 2026 spam update | September 24 | 13 days, 16 hours |

## What changed, in plain words?

Google runs automated systems that detect spam all the time, including SpamBrain, its AI-based spam system. A [spam update](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/spam-updates) is Google's name for a notable improvement to those systems. Sites that break the [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) may rank lower or disappear from results.

A spam update is not a manual action. A manual action is a penalty applied after human review, and Google reports it in Search Console's [Manual actions report](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/9044175). A spam update is automated and comes with no notice, so the only sign is a change in your traffic.

The policies cover practices such as scaled content abuse, expired domain abuse, site reputation abuse, link spam, cloaking and hacked content. Scaled content abuse is the one most relevant now: Google's examples include using generative AI tools to produce many pages without adding value for users.

## What does it mean for small businesses and founders?

Most small business sites have nothing to fear from a spam update. A site with real service pages, honest reviews and content written for customers is not what these systems look for.

The risk sits in shortcuts that are easy to take today. Think of a batch of AI-written location pages with only the city name changed. Or articles bought on another site to pass links, or an old domain bought for its backlinks. All of these fit Google's spam policies. So does a hacked WordPress site serving pages its owner never wrote. If an agency or freelancer did SEO work for you, ask what they built and where your links came from.

Recovery is slow. Google says a site can improve once its systems learn over a period of months that the site complies. For a link spam update, it says the ranking benefit lost from spammy links cannot be regained at all.

## What should you do now?

1. **Compare Google clicks** in the Search Console Performance report for the two weeks before September 24 and the two weeks after October 8. A drop that starts inside the rollout window is worth investigating.
2. **Check the Manual actions and Security issues reports** in Search Console, so you can rule out a penalty or a hack.
3. **Search `site:yourdomain.com`** on Google and look for pages you do not recognize.
4. **Review pages made in bulk** and either improve them with real, specific information or remove them.
5. **Do not rush to disavow links or rewrite everything.** Clean up what breaks the policies, then give it months, not days.
