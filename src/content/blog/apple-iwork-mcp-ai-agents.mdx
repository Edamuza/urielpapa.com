Apple is opening Keynote, Pages and Numbers to outside AI agents. On October 27, 2026, Apple announced that the three apps will support the Model Context Protocol (MCP), which "enables external AI agents and tools to read content, perform edits" and run automated actions in them. It arrives in beta with the Creator Studio updates in November 2026 and needs macOS 27 or later.

This take is part of my [Automation](/topics/automation) writing.

## What happened?

Apple announced the change in [its Creator Studio newsroom post](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/10/apple-creator-studio-adds-powerful-new-ai-features-and-even-more-creative-tools/). MCP is an open standard that lets AI agents connect to apps and data. Apple lists MCP support among the AI features it is introducing in beta, next to new assistants inside Keynote, Pages, Numbers and Logic Pro.

## What changed?

These points come from [Apple's announcement](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/10/apple-creator-studio-adds-powerful-new-ai-features-and-even-more-creative-tools/):

* **Agents can work in your documents.** Through MCP, outside AI agents and tools can read, edit and run actions in Keynote, Pages and Numbers. This requires macOS 27 or later.
* **Built-in assistants.** Keynote helps design and animate slides, Pages drafts and restructures text, and Numbers builds tables and recommends charts. Apple says they are "built on Apple models and OpenAI."
* **Beta and US English only.** The assistants are in beta, in US English only, and need an Apple Intelligence-capable device on iOS 27, iPadOS 27 or macOS 27.
* **Timing and price.** The updates reach the App Store in November. Creator Studio costs US$12.99 a month or US$129 a year in the US, and is free for existing subscribers.

Apple says free versions of Keynote, Pages and Numbers stay available, but it presents the new AI features as part of Creator Studio. It does not say whether the free apps get MCP.

## What does it mean for businesses and site owners?

This is a big step for small teams that live in Apple's office apps. Today, getting AI output into a Numbers sheet or a Keynote deck usually means copying and pasting. With MCP, an agent such as Claude could pull figures into a sheet, update a monthly report deck or tidy a proposal in Pages directly.

It also shows MCP becoming the common plug for AI tools, the same direction I covered with [Agent Skills moving to the Agentic AI Foundation](/news/agent-skills-agentic-ai-foundation). Access works both ways, though. An agent that can edit your files can also damage them, which is why I wrote about checking [MCP security in OpenCode](/news/opencode-mcp-oauth-rce).

## What should you do now?

* **Plan for macOS 27.** MCP support needs it, so check which of your Macs can upgrade.
* **Pick one workflow to test,** such as a weekly sales sheet or a report deck, once the beta arrives.
* **Work on copies first.** Let an agent edit duplicates of important files until you trust its changes.
