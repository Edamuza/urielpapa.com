OpenAI's frontier models and its Codex coding agent are coming to Google Cloud. OpenAI and Google Cloud announced on October 21, 2026 that the models will be sold through Google Cloud's Model Garden, with general availability planned in the coming weeks. For a business that already runs on Google Cloud, it means one more way to use OpenAI without a separate contract.

This take is part of my [AI, in Depth](/topics/ai) writing.

## What happened?

On October 21, 2026, OpenAI published [OpenAI models are coming to Google Cloud](https://openai.com/index/openai-models-google-cloud/). The two companies are partnering to bring OpenAI's frontier models and Codex agentic workflows into Model Garden, Google Cloud's catalogue of AI models. OpenAI says the models will also "soon be available" in Google Antigravity, Google's coding tool, and in the Gemini Agent.

## What changed, in plain words?

These are the main points from [OpenAI's announcement](https://openai.com/index/openai-models-google-cloud/):

* **One more place to buy OpenAI models.** Customers will buy OpenAI's frontier models in Model Garden, billed through the Google Cloud Marketplace.
* **Existing commitments count.** Companies with Google Cloud spend commitments will be able to draw them down for these purchases.
* **Google Cloud's controls apply.** Access runs with Google Cloud's access controls, central data governance and infrastructure.
* **Not live yet.** General availability is planned in the coming weeks, and interested customers can request access through a form on OpenAI's site.
* **Microsoft stays first.** OpenAI says Microsoft remains its primary cloud partner.

The announcement does not name the models, the regions or the prices, so those are still open questions.

## What does it mean for businesses and site owners?

For most small businesses calling the OpenAI API directly, nothing changes today. The difference is for companies that already run on Google Cloud. They can add OpenAI models to the same bill, under the same permissions, and spend a commitment they have already made instead of signing a new contract.

It also makes comparing models easier. A team can try OpenAI's models next to [Google's Gemini 4 Argon](/news/gemini-4-argon) from one console and pick by cost and quality per task. My post on [GPT-6 Sol and Luna](/news/gpt-6-sol-luna) covers OpenAI's current API prices, which are the baseline to check once Google Cloud publishes its own.

## What should you do now?

* **If you're on Google Cloud,** request access and wait for the model list and prices before you plan a move.
* **Check the price against the direct API.** Count your commitment drawdown, but compare the per-token price with OpenAI's own.
* **Expect changes to your code.** Calling a model through Google Cloud may use a different endpoint and login than the OpenAI API, so test before you switch.
