The Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) has issued Revenue Memorandum Circular (RMC) No. 98-2026, the detailed rules for electronic invoicing in the Philippines. Covered businesses, including e-commerce sellers above the Micro classification, must issue electronic invoices by December 31, 2026. The circular also makes clear that a spreadsheet invoice or a printed system receipt does not count.

I co-developed the [VITE SEO ERP](/projects/vite-seo-erp), whose accounting module handled invoices and BIR VAT reports, so I read rules like this as a builder: what the system must do, not only what the form must show. This take is part of my [Technology and Business](/topics/tech-and-business) writing, and my notes from the [Philippine SME Business Expo](/events/philippine-sme-business-expo-technology-adoption) cover how small businesses here adopt tools like these.

## What happened?

The BIR issued [RMC No. 98-2026](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RMC%20No.%2098-2026%20redacted.pdf) on September 22, 2026, and it took effect immediately. It sets policies and guidelines for issuing electronic invoices under Revenue Regulations No. 8-2022 and No. 11-2025, as amended by No. 26-2025. What is new is the detail on what an electronic invoice is and the steps to get there.

## What changed, in plain words?

The circular covers five groups: taxpayers in e-commerce or internet transactions classified as Small, Medium or Large; taxpayers under the Large Taxpayers Service; Large Taxpayers under the Ease of Paying Taxes Act; taxpayers using computerized accounting systems or other invoicing software; and others the Commissioner may require. Micro taxpayers are exempt.

An invoice counts as electronic only if it meets all three conditions:

* **Made by a registered system** in a structured electronic format, not typed by hand.
* **Sent to the buyer electronically,** for example by email, QR code, app or web link.
* **Ready for the BIR,** meaning its data can be extracted and sent for sales reporting. The BIR's system uses the JSON file format, and other formats are fine if the data can be converted.

The circular names what fails. Invoices made in Microsoft Word, Excel, Google Docs or Google Sheets are not valid electronic invoices. A printout from a POS or accounting system is not one either, unless that system can also send the invoice to the buyer and report sales data to the BIR.

Other rules matter for daily operations:

* **Permit first.** You need a Permit to Issue (PTI) Electronic Invoice from your Revenue District Office before issuing e-invoices. The BIR evaluates complete applications within 20 working days, and switching software needs a new or amended permit.
* **Certification within six months.** EIS Certification, an online test of your system, must follow within six months of the permit, or the permit can be revoked.
* **No edits.** Issued e-invoices cannot be changed. Decreases need a credit note, and increases need a new invoice.
* **Downtime plan.** If the system is down, issue a BIR-authorized manual invoice, then replace it with an e-invoice that references it.
* **Whole business.** The head office and all branches must comply.

Electronic sales reporting is a separate obligation that starts only when the BIR issues its own guidelines. Rules for e-invoicing service providers will also come in a separate issuance.

## What does it mean for small businesses and founders?

The biggest change is for small online sellers who are above the Micro classification. Many issue invoices from a spreadsheet template or a simple app, and the circular names those as invalid. They now need either invoicing software that can issue and transmit structured invoices or a provider that does it for them.

For founders building POS, accounting or e-commerce tools for Philippine businesses, this is a product requirement with a date. A system that prints receipts but cannot send invoices or produce BIR-ready data no longer helps a covered client comply, and it must also pass the BIR's certification tests.

## What should you do now?

1. **Confirm your classification.** Check whether you are Micro or above, and whether you sell online or use invoicing software.
2. **Ask your software vendor directly** whether its system can issue structured e-invoices, send them to buyers and export BIR-ready JSON data.
3. **Retire spreadsheet invoices** for any covered business before December 31, 2026.
4. **Apply for the PTI early,** given the 20-working-day review, then plan for EIS Certification within six months.
5. **Write a downtime procedure** with authorized manual invoices and a way to replace them later.
6. **Check the BIR's own issuances** for the separate rules on PTI procedures and service providers before you sign with any vendor.

- [Revenue Regulations No. 11-2025](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RR%20No.%2011-2025.pdf) - The BIR regulation that defines an electronic invoice and the Electronic Sales Reporting System, which RMC 98-2026 builds on.
- [Revenue Regulations No. 26-2025](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RR%20No.%2026-2025.pdf) - The short regulation that moved the e-invoicing deadline to December 31, 2026, and lists the taxpayers who follow later.
