Vercel launched Flat Rate CDN, a fixed monthly price for CDN traffic on its Pro plan, replacing pay-per-use billing for requests and data transfer. It matters because the main risk of usage-based hosting is a surprise bill after a traffic spike, and this plan is built to remove that risk for the parts of the bill it covers.

[TravelEase](/projects/travelease), the tourism portal my capstone team built, runs on Vercel, and the ERP I led and co-developed at my former agency deployed its Next.js front end there. This take is part of my [Tech Stacks](/topics/tech-stacks) writing.

## What happened?

Vercel announced [Flat Rate CDN](https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-flat-rate-cdn) on its blog on September 8, 2026. It is on by default for all new Pro teams, and existing Pro teams can opt in from their billing settings. The plan applies to the whole team, so it covers every project under it.

## What changed, in plain words?

Instead of paying for each request and gigabyte, a Pro team picks a tier and pays the same amount each month. These are the tiers from the [announcement](https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-flat-rate-cdn):

| Tier | CDN requests per month | Data transfer | Price |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Flat Rate CDN 1M | 1 million | 1 TB | Included with Pro |
| Flat Rate CDN 10M | 10 million | 50 TB | $20 a month |
| Flat Rate CDN 50M | 50 million | 50 TB | $100 a month |
| Flat Rate CDN 150M | 150 million | 50 TB | $300 a month |

The flat fee covers CDN requests, Fast Data Transfer, Blob Data Transfer and the observability events those requests create. Vercel says it detects short spikes and leaves them out when it compares your usage to your tier, and there are no hard caps, so a site stays up during viral traffic. If sustained usage is well above your tier, Vercel may move projects to its Flex CDN tier and resize your plan at the start of the next billing cycle. You get notifications as usage nears your tier's limit.

As of October 2026, Vercel's [pricing page](https://vercel.com/pricing) lists Pro at $20 a month per developer seat.

## What does it mean for small businesses and founders?

For a small business, a predictable bill is often worth more than the cheapest possible bill. A fixed CDN cost is easier to quote to a client, easier to budget and less stressful when a campaign or a news mention sends a wave of visitors. Vercel's own example is a team on the $20 tier whose viral launch would have cost "tens of thousands of dollars" under usage billing.

The limit is scope. The post lists what the flat fee covers and does not say it covers Functions or Image Optimization. An app that renders pages on the server, runs API routes or resizes images can still have usage-based costs on top of the flat rate. Static and cached pages benefit most, which is one more reason to keep pages static where you can.

## What should you do now?

* **On a Pro team already:** open the Usage page and check your typical monthly CDN requests and transfer. Opt in only if a tier fits your normal traffic, and remember you can opt out later.
* **Starting a new Pro team:** Flat Rate CDN is on by default, so pick the tier that matches expected traffic rather than leaving it to chance.
* **Running server-heavy apps:** review Functions and image costs separately, since the flat rate does not settle those.
* **Serving mostly static pages:** this plan rewards caching, so make sure marketing pages and blogs are served from the CDN.
