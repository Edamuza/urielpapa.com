Maya, the Philippine digital payments company, now lets merchants accept Apple Pay on Maya Terminal in stores and on Maya Checkout online. Maya says the change adds no Apple Pay acceptance fee and no extra merchant discount rate (MDR). For a small business, it is one more way to get paid without buying new equipment.

At the Philippine SME Business Expo, the tools owners adopted fastest were the ones that fixed a daily problem such as getting paid, which is why I follow payment changes closely. This take is part of my [Technology and Business](/topics/tech-and-business) writing, and my [notes from the Philippine SME Business Expo](/events/philippine-sme-business-expo-technology-adoption) cover that pattern in more detail.

## What happened?

Maya Business announced on August 17, 2026 that [Apple Pay is now available on Maya Terminal and Maya Checkout](https://www.maya.ph/business/stories/apple-pay-is-now-available-on-maya-terminal-and-maya-checkout). The story calls it "a long-awaited milestone" for merchants. A follow-up story on August 18, 2026, [Maya brings Apple Pay to Philippine merchants](https://www.maya.ph/business/stories/maya-brings-apple-pay-to-190000-philippine-merchants), repeats the details and adds that Maya Checkout works with Shopify and WooCommerce stores.

## What changed, in plain words?

Apple Pay is now one more payment method inside Maya's two main merchant products:

* **Maya Terminal** is the in-store card machine. It already took credit and debit cards, QR Ph and digital wallets. Customers can now tap a compatible Apple device to pay, and Maya says merchants "don't need another payment terminal machine" for this.
* **Maya Checkout** is the online payment gateway. Apple Pay is added through the same single integration that already handles cards, wallets and QR payments, so an online store does not need a new setup.
* **Fees.** Maya says there are "no additional Apple Pay acceptance fees, no additional merchant discount rate (MDR) charges." Existing settlement timelines do not change.
* **Security.** Maya says Apple Pay transactions get the same protections as other payments, citing PCI DSS-compliant security on Maya Terminal and fraud management on Maya Checkout.

Neither story describes a separate activation step for existing merchants, so it is worth confirming with Maya that Apple Pay is switched on for your account.

## What does it mean for small businesses and founders?

The main benefit is fewer lost sales at the counter. A customer who left a physical card at home but carries a compatible Apple device can still pay by tapping. Because the fee and settlement terms stay the same, accepting Apple Pay costs a Maya merchant nothing extra beyond its existing rate.

For online stores, the change matters most on mobile. A wallet payment saves the shopper from typing a card number on a small screen. Merchants on Shopify or WooCommerce with Maya Checkout get this through the integration they already run.

Founders building apps or booking systems for Philippine businesses should note the pattern. Payment options increasingly arrive through the processor a business already uses, not through a new contract. It fits what I saw at the expo: owners adopt technology faster when it arrives inside a tool they already trust.

## What should you do now?

1. **Confirm Apple Pay is active** on your Maya Terminal and Maya Checkout account with Maya support or your Maya Business dashboard.
2. **Test one real payment** with an Apple device in store and on your checkout page before you announce it.
3. **Tell staff** that tapping an Apple device is accepted, so they do not turn customers away.
4. **Add Apple Pay to your signage and checkout copy,** such as "We accept Apple Pay," so customers know before they reach the counter.
5. **Check your own fee terms.** Maya's stories say there is no extra MDR, but your existing rate still applies, so review it in your contract.
