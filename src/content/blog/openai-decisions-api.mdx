OpenAI's Decisions API is now generally available, giving apps a fast, low-cost way to get a yes-or-no probability, a choice or a score from a model instead of a paragraph of text. OpenAI published it on October 22, 2026, built on GPT-6 Luna. For anyone who uses AI to sort, route or score things, this is a cheaper tool for that job.

This take is part of my [AI, in Depth](/topics/ai) writing.

## What happened?

OpenAI announced the [Decisions API](https://openai.com/index/decisions-api/) with the tagline "Fast, focused, low-cost decisions for any application." The [developer guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/decisions) says it is generally available and returns answers "about 10x faster than the Responses API."

## What changed?

These points come from [OpenAI's announcement](https://openai.com/index/decisions-api/) and [the Decisions guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/decisions):

* **Three answer types.** A predicate returns the probability that a condition is true. A choice returns one option from a list you supply. A score returns a weighted score across 2 to 10 ordered levels.
* **One model.** gpt-6-luna is the only supported model, called through a POST request to /v1/decisions.
* **Input-only pricing.** Uncached input costs US$0.10 per million tokens, cached input is cheaper, and output tokens are not charged.
* **Bigger batches.** One request can carry up to 400 questions, up from 200 during the public beta.
* **Text, images and JSON.** Context can be text, images or native JSON.

OpenAI also lists early users. Sierra reported under 280 ms p95 latency, and Ramp reported being 4 times faster and 30% cheaper than GPT-6 Luna for attributing AI costs. Those are the companies' own numbers, published by OpenAI.

## What does it mean for businesses and site owners?

A lot of everyday AI work is a decision, not writing. Is this email a sales lead? Which team should get this ticket? Is this review spam? Until now, the usual way was to ask a chat model and parse its answer. The Decisions API gives back a typed answer and a probability, which is easier to use in an n8n or Zapier step and easier to set a threshold on.

The input-only pricing also changes the maths. You pay for what you send, not for what the model writes back, so short, repeated checks get cheap. My post on [GPT-6 Sol and Luna](/news/gpt-6-sol-luna) covers the model it runs on.

## What should you do now?

* **List your AI steps that are really decisions,** such as tagging, routing, filtering and scoring.
* **Test one against your current prompt.** Compare accuracy, speed and cost on a sample of real cases before you switch.
* **Set a confidence threshold.** Send low-probability answers to a person instead of acting on them.
