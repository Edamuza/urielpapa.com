At the end of this guide you will have an AI chatbot for Facebook Messenger and Instagram that answers inquiries, books consultations and logs leads automatically. It runs on n8n for the workflow, the Meta messaging platform for the two channels, and OpenRouter for the AI model.

I led and co-developed a chatbot like this at VITE SEO Digital Advertising, the agency I founded and have since closed, and this guide is based on that build. The steps below follow the order we built it in. It is part of my [Automation](/topics/automation) writing. We built it because inquiries from our [Meta ads](/blog/organic-content-vs-paid-ads) often came in at night and on weekends, and I wanted every person to get a reply right away instead of the next morning.

## What do you need before you start?

* **A Facebook Page**, and an Instagram professional account connected to it.
* **A Meta developer account** and an app with messaging access. The app needs the `pages_messaging` permission for Messenger and `instagram_manage_messages` for Instagram. Standard Access works for Pages and accounts you own or manage. To serve accounts you don't manage, Meta requires [Advanced Access, which needs App Review and Business Verification](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/overview).
* **An n8n instance**, either n8n Cloud or self-hosted, reachable over HTTPS so Meta can call its webhook.
* **An OpenRouter account and API key**, with credit for the model you pick.
* **A place to log leads**, such as a Google Sheet or a database table.
* **A booking link**, such as a calendar scheduling page.
* **A written list of what the bot should know**: services, prices, hours, FAQs and when to hand over to a person.

## How do you build it, step by step?

1. **Create a Meta app.** In [Meta for Developers](https://developers.facebook.com/apps/), create a business app, add the **Messenger** product, and connect your Facebook Page. Then open Messenger's Instagram settings and connect the Instagram professional account linked to that Page. Meta renames menus from time to time, so follow the current [Messenger Platform docs](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/messenger-platform) if a label differs.
2. **Connect the webhook to n8n.** Add a Webhook node in n8n, copy its production URL into the Meta app's webhook settings, and set a verify token. Add a branch that answers Meta's verification request so the webhook is accepted.
3. **Parse the incoming message.** Pull out the sender ID, the channel (Messenger or Instagram) and the message text. Ignore echoes of your own replies so the bot does not answer itself.
4. **Add the AI reply node through OpenRouter.** Use an HTTP Request node or an AI model node pointed at OpenRouter, pass the system prompt and the recent messages, and read back the reply.
5. **Write the system prompt.** Tell the model who it speaks for, what it knows, the tone to use, what it must not answer, and the exact phrase or flag to return when a human should take over.
6. **Add booking and logging branches.** When the person wants a consultation, send the booking link. For every conversation, write the sender, channel, message and outcome to your lead log.
7. **Send the reply.** Call the [Send API](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/messenger-platform/send-messages) with a `POST` to `https://graph.facebook.com/v25.0/{PAGE-ID}/messages`, using the Page access token. The body needs `recipient.id` (the sender's ID from the webhook), `messaging_type: "RESPONSE"` and `message.text`. You can reply freely within 24 hours of the person's last message. After that, Meta only allows specific message types.
8. **Test.** Message the Page and the Instagram account from a test account. Try normal questions, off-topic questions, a request for a human and a message in another language.
9. **Go live.** Switch the Meta app to live mode, complete any app review Meta requires, and watch the first real conversations closely.

Here is a typical layout, node by node, in the order a message passes through it:

1. **Webhook:** receives every request Meta sends.
2. **IF (verification):** if it is Meta's verification request, a Respond to Webhook node returns the challenge and the run stops.
3. **Respond to Webhook:** sends Meta a quick `200 OK` so it does not resend the message.
4. **Code:** pulls out the sender ID, channel and message text, and drops echoes of the bot's own replies.
5. **Google Sheets (read):** loads the last few messages from this sender for context.
6. **HTTP Request (OpenRouter):** sends the system prompt and recent messages, and reads back the reply and any flag.
7. **Switch:** routes on the flag: a normal answer, a booking request, or a handover.
8. **HTTP Request (Send API):** sends the reply, with the booking link added when the person wants a call.
9. **Google Sheets (append):** logs the sender, channel, message and outcome.
10. **Gmail:** on a handover, emails a person with the conversation so far.

<Callout type="tip">
  Keep the system prompt short and specific. It is sent with every message, so every extra sentence adds tokens to every reply. My post on [what tokenization is](/blog/what-is-tokenization) explains why that matters for cost.
</Callout>

## What does it cost to run?

The table below is a rough example budget for a small self-hosted setup, not a record of a real bill.


| Item                         | Monthly cost       | Notes                                                                                  |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| n8n hosting                  | About US$6         | Self-hosted on a small VPS (1 to 2 GB of memory)                                       |
| AI tokens through OpenRouter | About US$3 to US$5 | A low-cost model such as GPT-4o mini, around 6,000 to 10,000 tokens per conversation   |
| Lead log and booking tool    | About US$0 extra   | Google Sheets and a Google Calendar booking page, often already in a Google Workspace plan |
| Total                        | About US$10        | Rough estimate for a small setup, early 2026 prices                                  |

The biggest variable is usually tokens: how long the system prompt is, how much history you send with each message and how long the replies are.

## What does it do well, and where does it fall short?

| Handles well                                      | Falls short                                           | When a human must step in                               |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Common questions about services, prices and hours | Questions the system prompt does not cover            | The person asks for a human                             |
| Replies at any hour                               | Messages with little context, such as a single emoji  | Complaints or sensitive topics                          |
| Sending the booking link at the right moment      | Long back-and-forth negotiations                      | Anything involving payments, contracts or personal data |
| Logging every lead in one place                   | Knowing when information in the prompt is out of date | When the bot is unsure and says so                      |

Our chatbot did best with the simple, repeated questions: what services we offered, rough package prices and how to book a call. It struggled with mixed Taglish messages and very short ones like "hm" or "how much?", where it sometimes guessed which service the person meant. It also needed a prompt update every time our packages changed, so I learned to treat the system prompt like a price list someone has to keep current.

## When should a person take over?

Build the handover in from the start. Have the model return a clear flag when it should not answer, then have n8n stop replying in that conversation and notify a person, for example by email or a chat message. Tell the customer that someone will follow up, and keep the logged conversation so the person does not have to ask everything again.

## What should you check before going live?

* The webhook answers Meta's verification request.
* The bot ignores its own messages.
* The system prompt lists what the bot must not answer.
* Handover to a person works and sends a notification.
* Every conversation is logged with its channel and outcome.
* You know your expected monthly token use and have set a spending limit where your provider allows it.
* Your privacy notice covers storing messages and contact details.
