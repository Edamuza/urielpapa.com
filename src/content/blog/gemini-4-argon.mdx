Google has released Gemini 4 Argon, its new frontier model for coding, long multi-step tasks and business documents. It was announced on September 30, 2026 and opened to paying developers on October 13. For a small business, the key facts are the price, which doubles on December 1, and the lack of a free tier.

This take is part of my [AI, in Depth](/topics/ai) writing. If per-token pricing is new to you, my explainer on [what tokenization is and why it decides what AI costs](/blog/what-is-tokenization) covers how these bills add up.

## What happened?

Google DeepMind announced the model in [Gemini 4 Argon: our next era of frontier intelligence](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-argon/), a post by Koray Kavukcuoglu dated September 30, 2026. Access started with trusted cyber defenders in Google's [Fairwind Program](https://deepmind.google/fairwind-program/), with a phased rollout after that.

On October 13, Google said [Gemini 4 Argon is now available for developers](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/build-with-gemini-4-argon/) on the paid tier of the Gemini API, in Google AI Studio and in Android Studio. A day later, the [Google Cloud announcement](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/gemini-4-argon-available-on-google-cloud) added Gemini for business on the web, desktop and mobile.

## What changed, in plain words?

* **A new top model.** Gemini 4 Argon sits above the Flash models as Google's most capable model so far. The model ID is `gemini-4-argon`.
* **Much longer answers.** The output limit rises to 1 million tokens, up from 64K, according to the launch post. The [Gemini models page](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/models) lists a 1M context window.
* **Strong scores, self-reported.** Google reports 77.9 percent on the DeepSWE v1.1 coding benchmark and first place on Zapier's AutomationBench at 51.3 percent. These are Google's own figures.
* **A two-step price.** The [Gemini API pricing page](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing) lists US$2 input and US$10 output per million tokens through November 30, 2026, then US$4 and US$20 from December 1. Cached input is US$0.10, then US$0.20. Batch requests cost half.
* **No free tier.** Argon is paid-only in the Gemini API.
* **Cyber limits.** Offensive security tasks are blocked by default. An unrestricted version goes only to vetted defenders through Fairwind.

## What does it mean for small businesses and founders?

Gemini 4 Argon is a premium model, and most small-business tasks do not need it. Gemini 3.8 Flash costs US$0.75 input and US$3.75 output per million tokens through December 31, 2026, and it keeps a free tier. Argon already costs about 2.7 times as much at its introductory rate, and more than five times as much after December 1.

Here is a rough estimate using the chatbot volume from my tokenization post: about 2 million input tokens and 150,000 output tokens a month. On Argon's introductory rate, that is 2 × US$2 plus 0.15 × US$10, or about US$5.50 a month. From December 1 it becomes 2 × US$4 plus 0.15 × US$20, or about US$11. On Gemini 3.8 Flash it is about US$2.06. These are estimates, not a bill, and costs grow with volume.

Argon earns its price on long, multi-step work. Google Cloud's post describes reading mixed documents with tables and charts, ledger reconciliations, and multi-file code changes. It also says the model is trained to pause for confirmation before irreversible steps. I use Gemini for research and reading long documents, so the long output limit is the change I would test first.

## What should you do now?

* **Test on your own tasks.** Run a handful of real prompts on Argon and on 3.8 Flash, and compare the answers before you switch anything.
* **Budget at the December price.** A feature that is cheap on the introductory rate will cost twice as much from December 1, 2026.
* **Keep Flash for volume.** Customer replies, tagging and short summaries rarely need a frontier model.
* **Plan for paid access.** Argon is not on the free tier, so prototypes need a billed Gemini API project.

Prices here were checked in October 2026, and Google changes them often. Check the pricing page before you commit.
