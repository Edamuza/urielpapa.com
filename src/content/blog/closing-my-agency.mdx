VITE SEO Digital Advertising closed after financial losses, and losing it was one of the hardest things I have gone through as a founder. It taught me that a business is a set of promises, that I should make fewer of them and keep them better, and that I have to protect what I genuinely have.

This story is part of my writing on [The Founder's Journey](/topics/founder-journey). I am telling it honestly and from my own side. I will not name clients or team members, and I am still learning from it as I write.

## How did the agency start?

I founded VITE SEO Digital Advertising at 21, while still an undergraduate at the Technological University of the Philippines, after two years of freelance work for the same international marketing clients. Before that, I had worked as a WordPress developer, a team leader and a project manager for those clients.

I turned freelance work into a company because the work had grown bigger than one person. Clients kept coming back, and I was already leading a small team for them. A company felt like the honest next step. I hoped it would become a place where young Filipino developers and marketers could do good work for clients around the world, and where we could build our own products too.

## What did we build together?

The agency grew to a team of up to 22 across sales, development, project management, marketing and SEO. As founder and CEO, I directed in-house product development:

* An internal ERP that put HR, CRM, project management and accounting behind one login, built with [Laravel for the API and Next.js for the interface](/blog/laravel-vs-nextjs).
* An NFC business card product, the "Tap into Possibilities" card.
* A client quotation portal.
* 5 WordPress SEO plugins.

I also built the sales process (a four-tier service package line, a consultation playbook, a client fit-check form, proposals and quotations), and wrote the SOPs, job descriptions and employment contracts the team worked from. We also ran our own marketing, both Meta ads and organic content, and I compare the two in [organic content vs paid ads](/blog/organic-content-vs-paid-ads).

I was proudest of the team and the systems we built for ourselves. Most days started with client calls and sales messages, then moved to task check-ins, code reviews and planning. Many nights went to building. It was busy and tiring, but I was learning something new almost every day.

## What was it like leading a team while still in college?

Leading a team while still in college meant living on two schedules at once. I went to classes and did school projects, then ran the business around them, often late at night. Some of my team were my age and some were older than me. I learned that respect comes from being clear and fair, not from a title. I also learned to write things down. Clear SOPs, clear tasks and honest feedback helped the team more than anything I said in a meeting.

## Why did the agency close?

The agency closed because of financial losses. The business could no longer carry itself, and I had to accept that it was over. I am not going to pretend it was a clean, planned decision to move on to something better. It was a loss, and it hurt.

## What was it like when it was gone?

Losing the agency was devastating. The company had become part of my life: my days, my plans and much of who I thought I was were built around it. When it was gone, moving forward was hard, and for a while I struggled to see what came next.

The hardest part was looking back honestly. Some of what went wrong came from my own mistakes in business, and realizing that was a harsh truth. It is easier to blame timing or the market, but I learned more once I stopped doing that and looked at my own decisions.

## What changed for me as an entrepreneur?

As an entrepreneur, I now see every commitment as something I have to carry, not just something I can win. I take risks more slowly. I want to know what a decision will cost me in time and energy, not only in money. I am more careful about which clients and projects I say yes to, and I would rather do fewer things well. I also pay closer attention to simple numbers, like what comes in and what goes out each month.

## What changed for me as a technical founder?

As a technical founder, I learned that building something is not the same as selling it. We could build a full ERP in under 3 months, but code alone did not make the business stronger. Now I choose what to build by asking who needs it and what problem it solves first. Many of the problems I faced were about people, process or focus, and no new tool could fix them. Technology works best when it serves a clear need.

## What would I tell someone starting a business in college?

I would tell them to start small, protect their studies and be clear about why they are doing it.

* **Start smaller than you think.** Prove the work with a few clients before you hire a team.
* **Protect your studies.** School ends, and a good finish opens doors you will want later.
* **Write things down early.** Your processes, your prices and your promises should not live only in your head.
* **Know your reason.** When you know why you started, you also know when it is time to change course.

## What am I doing now?

I graduated cum laude with a BS in Information Systems from the Technological University of the Philippines in August 2026, and I am now writing here about technology, automation and what I learn along the way.

Now I am trying to rebuild myself and make the most of what I have. I want to protect the things I genuinely have now, and grow from there slowly and carefully. I am sharing that journey here, from the code I write to the lessons I am still learning. If you have lost a business, or are close to it, I hope it helps to know you are not the only one.
