Google announced on October 15, 2026 that AI Mode and AI Overviews now show more inline links, placed higher in each answer. It is also testing a section for official websites and widening its news carousels. For site owners, it means AI answers built to send more clicks out. Pages that state a clear fact or own a clear task are best placed to get them.

This take is part of my [SEO, GEO and AEO](/topics/seo-geo-aeo) writing. How AI search picks its sources in the first place is covered in [GEO and AEO fundamentals](/blog/geo-and-aeo-fundamentals).

## What happened?

Hema Budaraju, Vice President of Product Management at Google, announced the changes on October 15, 2026. The post, [More ways to connect with the web from Search](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/updates-ai-mode-overviews-explore-web/), is on Google's Keyword blog and lists five updates across Google's AI Search features. Preferred Sources lets searchers pick news sites they want to see more of. Google says the number of people using it has grown more than 7x since the start of the year.

## What changed, in plain words?

The five updates, from Google's announcement:

* **More inline links.** Inline links are links attached to a word or phrase inside the AI answer. Google says it has substantially increased how often they appear, and it is now showing them higher up in responses more often.
* **An official websites section (a test).** Some tasks have only one or two official sites that can help. For those, Google is testing a section at the top of the response that lists the links and says why each one is useful.
* **News carousels in more places.** The carousels of timely articles in AI Search are rolling out to more locations and languages, and they highlight a searcher's Preferred Sources.
* **Bigger related-article links.** The links at the end of some AI responses get image thumbnails and a more clickable design.
* **Clickable creator names.** On video results in AI Mode, Google started testing creator and publisher names that link to their [Search profiles](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/16904498).

Google did not say which countries get the inline link and official-site changes first. Two of the five items are labeled tests, so they may change or disappear.

## What does it mean for small businesses and founders?

Inline links attach to a specific phrase, so the passage that states a fact plainly is the one that earns the link. Picture a page that answers the question in its first two sentences, names the entity and cites a source. It is easier to link from a single phrase than a page that builds up to its point. That is the same passage habit I described in my GEO and AEO post, and this update makes it more valuable.

The official websites test matters most for businesses whose customers come to do something: book, pay, apply, check a requirement. If Google can tell that your page is the official place for that task, it may sit at the top of the answer. That depends on Google being sure which site is yours, so a consistent business name, address and contact details across your site and profiles help.

The creator profile change has a catch for readers here. As of October 2026, Google's help page says Search profiles are only available in Canada, India and the United States. They also require at least 10,000 followers on one platform, such as YouTube or Instagram. Most Philippine small businesses cannot use them yet.

## What should you do now?

* **Record a baseline this week.** Open the Generative AI performance report in Search Console and note impressions for your top pages, so you can compare after the rollout. The [report's help page](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16984139) says it counts impressions, and that its data comes from the Web search type in the main Performance report.
* **Rewrite the first two sentences of your key pages** so each one states one clear fact with the name of the thing it is about.
* **Make your official task pages obvious.** One page per task, a clear title, and the same business details everywhere.
* **Check clicks, not only impressions.** Compare AI impressions with Web clicks in the Performance report. Then look at referrals in GA4, as I describe in [how to check if ChatGPT cites your site](/blog/how-to-check-if-chatgpt-cites-your-site).
