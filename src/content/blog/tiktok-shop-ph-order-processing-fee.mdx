TikTok Shop Philippines has ended its discount on the order processing fee, so sellers again pay the regular ₱5 for each successfully delivered order. The change took effect on September 1, 2026, after a preferential rate that ran for nine months. Five pesos sounds small, but on low-priced items sold in volume it comes straight out of the margin.

I write about marketing for small businesses from the side of someone who has paid for reach and built it organically, and platform fees decide how much of either you can afford. This take is part of my [Marketing](/topics/marketing) writing.

## What happened?

TikTok Shop's official [Order Processing Fee policy for the Philippines](https://seller-ph.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=6374416701966081), updated on August 18, 2026, says the platform "will discontinue Order Processing Fee discounts starting from 00:00 September 1, 2026," Philippine time. Sellers had a preferential rate for nine consecutive months starting December 1, 2025. The official page does not state the discounted amount. Seller-tools company BigSeller [reported it as ₱3 per order](https://www.bigseller.com/blog/articleDetails/tiktok-shop-order-processing-fee-philippines-2026.4924.htm), so the increase is likely ₱2 per order.

## What changed, in plain words?

From September 1, 2026, the fee works like this, according to TikTok Shop's policy page:

* **Amount.** ₱5, tax inclusive, per successfully delivered order. It is the same whatever the order total or the number of items in it.
* **When it is charged.** It is deducted from your settlement when a delivered order is settled.
* **Returns.** If a delivered order is later returned or refunded, the fee is not refunded.
* **Waiver for existing sellers.** Sellers whose first product listing is more than 90 days old get their first 50 orders each calendar month waived.
* **Waiver for new sellers.** Sellers whose first listing was submitted within the past 90 days get all orders waived for the following calendar month.
* **Timing of waivers.** TikTok Shop checks eligibility on the 25th of each month and applies the waiver in the next month.

## What does it mean for small businesses and founders?

The fee hits low-priced items hardest. On a ₱99 product, ₱5 is about 5 percent of the price, before commission, payment fees, vouchers and shipping. On a ₱1,500 item, it barely shows. Because the fee is per order, not per item, bundles and minimum-order deals now earn back more of it.

The 50-order monthly waiver protects very small sellers. A shop with 300 delivered orders a month pays for 250 of them, which is ₱1,250. A shop under 50 orders a month pays nothing extra. Returns also cost more than they look, since the fee stays even when the buyer sends the item back.

The bigger lesson is that marketplace costs can change on short notice. TikTok Shop posted this update about two weeks before it took effect. Owners who check their real cost per order each month can adjust prices calmly instead of finding a smaller payout later. Paid reach on a platform works the same way, as I describe in my comparison of [organic content and paid ads](/blog/organic-content-vs-paid-ads).

## What should you do now?

1. **Check your September settlement** in Seller Center and confirm the ₱5 deduction on delivered orders after your waiver.
2. **Recalculate margin on low-priced items,** adding ₱5 per order to commission, payment fees and voucher costs.
3. **Encourage bigger orders** with bundles or minimum-spend free shipping, since the fee is charged once per order.
4. **Reduce avoidable returns** with clearer photos, sizes and descriptions, because the fee is not refunded on returned orders.
5. **Watch the policy page** for future changes, so price updates are planned, not rushed.
