Microsoft 365 has been partly down since the afternoon of November 12, 2026 (US time), and users may be unable to connect to Exchange Online, Outlook, Teams, SharePoint Online and Microsoft 365 Copilot. Microsoft says a core process on its servers is getting overwhelmed and taking capacity offline. Servers Microsoft brought back have been failing again under traffic, so the outage runs into Friday's workday in the Philippines.

This take is part of my [Technology and Business](/topics/tech-and-business "Technology and Business articles") writing.

## What happened?

Microsoft's [Microsoft 365 Status account](https://x.com/MSFT365Status "Microsoft 365 Status on X") first reported trouble with Exchange Online mailboxes in the Americas at 17:54 UTC on November 12. The problem soon grew into a wider incident, MO1550821, covering several services. A separate Teams issue earlier that day (TM1550488) was already resolved by then.

Microsoft posted the first Exchange alert on X:

<SocialPost platform="x" url="https://x.com/MSFT365Status/status/2120938282204688468" author="Microsoft 365 Status" handle="MSFT365Status" date="2026-11-12" text={"We're investigating reports of users experiencing issues accessing Exchange Online mailboxes through multiple connection methods in the Americas. More details can be found in the Microsoft 365 admin center under EX1550543."} />

Microsoft first blamed elevated load, then said traffic had not increased. [Tom's Guide's live coverage](https://www.tomsguide.com/news/live/microsoft-outlook-down-live-updates-outage-11-12-26 "Tom's Guide live updates on the November 12 Microsoft 365 outage") quotes Microsoft saying the load came from "a large number of servers being unexpectedly unavailable." The same coverage says Outlook reports on Downdetector peaked at about 130,000.

## Who is affected?

Anyone on Microsoft 365 for Business or Enterprise may be affected, in any region. Microsoft's [service health page](https://status.cloud.microsoft/ "Microsoft 365 service health status page") lists these services as potentially impacted:

- Exchange Online (Outlook email), Microsoft Teams and SharePoint Online
- Microsoft 365 Copilot, Planner, To Do and Intune
- Microsoft Defender XDR and Defender for Office 365
- Several Microsoft Purview services, including Data Loss Prevention
- Power BI, where exporting reports with sensitivity labels to PDF may fail

Microsoft revised this list at 01:01 UTC on November 13, so check the status page for the current one.

The outage also reaches AI tools. [Security Affairs reports](https://securityaffairs.com/202932/security/microsoft-cloud-outage-hits-outlook-teams-copilot-and-ai-services.html "Security Affairs on the Microsoft cloud outage and its effect on AI services") that OpenAI and Anthropic both flagged failing Microsoft 365 connectors, so ChatGPT and Claude could not reach users' inboxes either.

## How serious is it?

Serious, and longer than a typical blip. By 22:09 UTC, Microsoft said its "actions taken thus far have not provided the anticipated relief." It also said it is reviewing a small number of recent configuration updates as possible contributors.

At 23:04 UTC (7:04 a.m. Friday in Manila), Microsoft said it was returning servers to service and [observing limited recovery](https://x.com/MSFT365Status/status/2121016338181161457 "Microsoft 365 Status post on limited recovery, 23:04 UTC").

That recovery did not hold. At 00:06 UTC (8:06 a.m. in Manila), Microsoft said the returned servers can fail again once traffic reaches them, and it is now rebuilding some servers with a previous configuration:

<SocialPost platform="x" url="https://x.com/MSFT365Status/status/2121031935010951521" author="Microsoft 365 Status" handle="MSFT365Status" date="2026-11-13" text={"The servers we returned to service can become unresponsive again once they receive traffic; we're analyzing diagnostic data to determine what's triggering the behavior. In parallel, we're temporarily limiting which types of traffic can reach the affected infrastructure in some locations. Additionally, we're rebuilding a subset of affected servers using a previous configuration to validate if this prevents them from becoming unresponsive. More info under EX1550543 and MO1550821 in the Microsoft 365 admin center, as well as http://status.cloud.microsoft if the admin center is not available to you."} />

Downdetector reports have dropped from the peak but not gone away. [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/news/live/microsoft-outlook-down-live-updates-outage-11-12-26 "Tom's Guide live updates on the November 12 Microsoft 365 outage") still counted about 30,000 Outlook reports at 7:50 p.m. Eastern Time on November 12 (8:50 a.m. in Manila).

## What does it mean for small businesses and founders?

My read: if your business runs on Outlook and Teams, this is a morning where your main tools may not work, and there is nothing you can fix on your side. The outage began overnight in the Philippines, so many teams will meet it when they log in on Friday.

It also shows how much now sits on one account. Email, files, chat, security tools and AI assistants all went down together. A business with no second way to reach clients feels that the most.

## What should you do now?

1. **Check the status, not your settings.** Look at [Microsoft's service health page](https://status.cloud.microsoft/ "Microsoft 365 service health status page") or Health in the Microsoft 365 admin center before you reset passwords or reinstall Outlook.
2. **Tell clients and staff early.** Post a short note on your other channels (Viber, Messenger, SMS) that email may be delayed today.
3. **Don't resend everything.** Microsoft has not said mail is lost. Wait for recovery, then follow up only on time-sensitive messages.
4. **Keep files you need today offline.** If OneDrive or SharePoint is synced on your laptop, work from the local copy.
5. **Write down your backup channel.** After today, agree with your team which tool you use when Microsoft 365 is down.
