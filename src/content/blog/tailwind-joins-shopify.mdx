Tailwind Labs, the company behind the Tailwind CSS framework, is joining Shopify. The free framework stays open source under the MIT license, but the paid products, Tailwind Plus and ui.sh, are closed to new customers. For most teams nothing breaks today, and the bigger change is who pays for Tailwind's future.

Tailwind CSS styles this site, [TravelEase](/projects/travelease) and the [NFC card platform](/projects/nfc-card-platform) I led and co-developed, so I read this one closely. It is part of my [Tech Stacks](/topics/tech-stacks) writing.

## What happened?

On September 9, 2026, Tailwind creator Adam Wathan published [Tailwind is joining Shopify](https://tailwindcss.com/blog/tailwind-is-joining-shopify) on the official Tailwind CSS blog. The post says the goal is to give Tailwind a stable long-term home where it will be actively maintained. It notes that Tailwind is installed over 110 million times per week and that Shopify was one of the first large companies to adopt it. The post gives no financial terms or closing date.

## What changed, in plain words?

These are the changes the [announcement](https://tailwindcss.com/blog/tailwind-is-joining-shopify) describes:

* **The framework stays open.** The post says nothing changes with Tailwind CSS or the company's other open-source projects, and that "everything will always be MIT-licensed."
* **The same team keeps leading it.** The Tailwind team will continue to lead and maintain the projects, now with Shopify's support.
* **The paid side stops growing.** Tailwind Labs says it will no longer try to grow a business around Tailwind.
* **No new customers for paid products.** Existing customers keep access to Tailwind Plus and ui.sh, but sign-ups for new customers are closed.

## What does it mean for small businesses and founders?

For most teams, the framework itself is the part that matters, and it is the part that stays the same. Tailwind CSS is free, MIT-licensed code that you install from npm. A change of owner cannot take back the versions you already use, and the license lets anyone keep using and changing them.

The funding change is arguably good news for upkeep. Until now, Tailwind Labs funded its work with the template business it built around the framework, as Wathan describes it. Now a large company that relies on Tailwind for its own products is backing the team. That makes long-term maintenance more likely, though the post does not say how Shopify's needs will shape the roadmap.

The real loss is for teams that planned to buy Tailwind Plus. If your agency or startup counted on its ready-made components and templates for new projects, that option is closed unless you already have access. Teams in that position will need another component source or will build their own.

## What should you do now?

* **Using Tailwind CSS:** no action needed. Keep upgrading on your normal schedule and watch the official blog for roadmap news.
* **Already a Tailwind Plus customer:** keep your account details safe, and download or save the components you use into your own repository, so a project does not depend on access you cannot buy again.
* **Planning to buy Tailwind Plus:** budget time for another component library or for building your own set.
* **Choosing a CSS approach now:** I would still pick Tailwind CSS for a new site. The license and the maintenance promise are what count, and both stay in place.
