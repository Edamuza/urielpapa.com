If your website runs on Apache, check its version today. Singapore's Cyber Security Agency (CSA) warned on November 17, 2026 that attackers are reportedly exploiting a flaw in Apache HTTP Server's HTTP/2 module that can let them run code on the server. Apache 2.4.71 fixes it. If you are on shared hosting, ask your host to confirm they have updated.

This take is part of my [tech stacks](/topics/tech-stacks "Tech stacks articles") writing. It follows my take on [php2shell, two critical PHP flaws](/news/php2shell-critical-php-vulnerabilities "php2shell: two critical PHP flaws and what site owners should do"), which hit the same kind of servers.

## What happened?

In [alert AL-2026-164](https://www.csa.gov.sg/alerts-and-advisories/alerts/al-2026-164/ "CSA alert AL-2026-164 on Apache mod_http2"), CSA said: "This vulnerability is reportedly being actively exploited, and a proof-of-concept exploit is publicly available." It applies to "Apache HTTP Server versions 2.4.55 to before 2.4.71 with mod_http2 enabled."

## What changed?

Apache's [security page for 2.4](https://httpd.apache.org/security/vulnerabilities_24.html "Apache HTTP Server 2.4 vulnerabilities") lists the flaw as CVE-2026-119618, rated critical by Apache: "mod_http2: heap corruption and RCE in assign_headers." CSA rates it high, with a CVSS score of 8.1.

- **Affected:** Apache HTTP Server 2.4.55 through 2.4.70, and mod_h2 2.0.8 through 2.0.42 from GitHub.
- **Fixed:** version 2.4.71, released on November 11, 2026.
- **Found by:** Anthropic's open-source scanner, reported on October 26, and Google Cloud's vulnerability researchers, reported on November 3.
- **Also fixed in 2.4.71:** a mod_md crash rated important (2.4.30 to 2.4.70) and a low-rated mod_vhost_alias bug.

Neither CSA nor Apache lists a workaround. The fix is the update.

## What does it mean for businesses and founders?

My read is that this is a "do it this week" item, not a "watch and wait" one. A remote code execution flaw with public exploit code is the kind attackers scan for across the whole internet, and small business sites are scanned like everyone else.

Who has to act depends on where your site lives. On a VPS or your own server, you or your developer update Apache. On shared or managed hosting, the host updates it, and your job is to ask. If your site runs on Nginx or a hosted platform like Shopify, this flaw does not apply to it.

## What should you do now?

1. **Find out if you run Apache.** Ask your developer or host, or run `apachectl -v` on your server.
2. **Check the version.** Anything from 2.4.55 to 2.4.70 needs the update to 2.4.71.
3. **Update, or ask your host to.** On shared hosting, open a ticket asking them to confirm Apache 2.4.71 or later.
4. **Watch your server.** Look for unknown files or new admin accounts if you were on an affected version.
