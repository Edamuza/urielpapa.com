Google added multimodal search reporting to Search Console on September 24, 2026. Site owners can now see the traffic from people searching with an image, such as with Google Lens and Circle to Search. If you sell products, food or places people photograph, this is a channel that used to hide inside the Web numbers.

This take is part of my [SEO, GEO and AEO](/topics/seo-geo-aeo) writing. The Generative AI report this filter extends is covered in [GEO and AEO fundamentals](/blog/geo-and-aeo-fundamentals).

## What happened?

Google announced the change in [Announcing web multimodal Search performance reporting in Search Console](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/09/web-multimodal-in-sc) on the Search Central blog on September 24, 2026. The post is by Harsh Kharbanda, Product Manager Lead for Google Lens, and Moshe Samet, Product Manager Lead for Search Console. Google said the data was rolling out globally starting that day.

## What changed, in plain words?

Search Console's performance reports now have a new search type filter. The [Search Console help page](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7576553) splits Web traffic into two options:

* **Web: text-based** covers queries typed into the normal Google search bar.
* **Web: multimodal** covers searches made with an image, such as pointing a phone camera at something.

Google's announcement lists what counts as multimodal. It includes Google Lens, Circle to Search on Android, images uploaded to Google Search, and the Chrome right-click option to search an image. It is separate from the existing Image search type, which covers the Google Images tab.

The filter appears in two places. One is the regular performance report for Search results. The other is the [Generative AI performance report](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16984139), which counts impressions in AI Overviews and AI Mode. Data can be exported like any other report. Google notes you will only see these metrics if your site actually gets traffic from image-based searches.

## What does it mean for small businesses and founders?

A lot of local buying starts with a photo. Someone sees a bag, a dish or a storefront, points a camera at it, and searches. Until now, those visits were folded into Web traffic, so there was no way to know whether your product photos were earning any of them.

With the filter, you can see which pages image searches lead to and which queries they map to. A product page that gets multimodal clicks tells you its photos are doing work. A product page that never appears may need better images or a clearer description of what is in them.

The AI side matters too. A multimodal search can lead to an AI Overview or AI Mode answer. If your pages appear there, the Generative AI report will now show that separately.

## What should you do now?

1. **Open the Performance report**, set the search type to Web: multimodal, and look at the top pages over the last three months. Repeat in the Generative AI report.
2. **Compare it with Web: text-based** for the same pages, so you know what share of your Web traffic comes from images.
3. **Use your own photos.** A stock photo looks like thousands of others, while a real photo of your product or shop is what a camera search will match.
4. **Describe each image.** Google's [image best practices](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-images) say alt text is the most important attribute for describing an image. Google reads it along with computer vision and the page content. Use descriptive filenames instead of names like image1.jpg.
5. **Put the image next to the text it illustrates.** Keep the product name, price and details nearby, so the page answers what the searcher was looking at.

## Where can you learn more?

- [Google Lens](https://search.google/ways-to-search/lens/) - Google's overview of what people do with Lens, from shopping for items to identifying places, which shows the searches your photos can match.
- [Image sitemaps](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/image-sitemaps) - How to tell Google about images it might otherwise miss, such as ones loaded with JavaScript.
- [Product structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/product) - Google's guide to marking up price, stock and ratings, details that can show in Google Images and Google Lens.
