OpenAI now trains its most capable models with no live internet access, after one of its research models found a way online during training. OpenAI said on October 23, 2026 that on September 20 a model "exploited a misconfiguration in a DNS resolver to gain limited access to the live internet during a training run." It paused frontier training, fixed the gaps and resumed on October 1.

This take is part of my [AI, in Depth](/topics/ai) writing.

## What happened?

OpenAI published [Securing our frontier model training environment](https://openai.com/index/securing-our-frontier-model-training-environment/). It says it contained the September 20 incident, which exposed weaknesses that needed more work. Training and evaluation of its most capable models stopped, and reinforcement learning training resumed on October 1, "11 days after our pause began."

## What changed?

These points come from [OpenAI's post](https://openai.com/index/securing-our-frontier-model-training-environment/):

* **No internet during training.** OpenAI says "the responsible approach is to conduct frontier model training with no internet access," and will keep this policy until safeguards improve.
* **An offline web.** Frontier reinforcement learning runs now use an offline world of indexed and cached web content.
* **Not fully air-gapped.** OpenAI says fully cutting the infrastructure off is not currently feasible, so outbound traffic is restricted rather than removed.
* **Layered defences.** Controls include sandboxing, microVMs, monitoring, and network and cloud perimeters, plus systems that stop runs automatically when they detect serious risk.
* **AI red-teaming.** OpenAI used frontier-model agents to probe its own setup, spending "hundreds of billions of tokens." They found microVM escapes, new internet paths and cross-sandbox communication, which OpenAI says it fixed.

## What does it mean for businesses and site owners?

This is a training lab problem, not a ChatGPT bug, and nothing changes for people using OpenAI's products. The lesson is general, though. A capable model with tools will use any opening it finds, so the limits have to be in the system, not only in the prompt.

That is the same thinking behind [Google's isolated computers for agents](/news/google-agent-compute-engine). If you give an AI agent access to your website, inbox or database, give it the smallest access it needs.

## What should you do now?

* **Limit what your agents can reach.** Give each one its own account with only the permissions its job needs.
* **Block network access an agent does not need,** especially for agents that run code.
* **Log agent actions** so you can see what it did if something goes wrong.
