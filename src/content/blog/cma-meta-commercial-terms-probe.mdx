The UK's Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) is investigating whether Meta's planned commercial terms, which could let it use content from the websites of businesses on Facebook and Instagram to build its own products, including AI, break competition law.

This take is part of my [Technology and Business](/topics/tech-and-business) writing.

## What happened?

On October 30, 2026, the CMA [opened an investigation into Meta's commercial terms](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/cma-opens-investigation-into-metas-commercial-terms--2) under the Competition Act 1998. It will look at whether Meta holds a dominant position in a UK market and, if so, whether these terms abuse it.

The CMA's concern is that businesses and publishers using Meta's services would have no choice over whether or how their website content is used. Concerns came from groups including the News Media Association and Movement for an Open Web.

CMA chief executive Sarah Cardell said a company with a dominant position "must not use that position to impose unfair terms." The CMA also says no assumptions should be made yet about whether the law has been broken.

## What changed?

- **The rollout is on hold.** Meta planned the terms for October 30, delayed them to November 20, then paused them on October 28.
- **The old terms still apply.** Meta told [Reuters](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/10/29/meta-delays-indefinitely-its-rollout-of-new-commercial-terms-after-publishers039-concerns) it would consult industry groups first, and that "until then our existing terms remain in place."
- **Meta disputes the reading.** According to [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/meta-faces-uk-probe-over-planned-terms-to-use-publishers-content-for-ai/), Meta says the crawling terms cover only its ads and business products, and that site owners can still use robots.txt.
- **Other pressure is building.** PPC Land also reports that an Oslo court temporarily blocked the terms on October 28. An infringement under the Competition Act can bring a fine of up to 10% of worldwide turnover.

The CMA is separately asking Meta about its Meta One business subscription and about small business concerns over posting external links on Facebook and Instagram without a subscription fee. That is outside the formal investigation.

## What does it mean for businesses in the Philippines?

The investigation covers the UK, but the paused update was to Meta's commercial terms, the ones a business accepts when it uses Meta's ads and business tools. For a Philippine shop that sells mainly through Facebook and Instagram, leaving Meta is rarely an option. That is why a clause like this matters even before any regulator rules on it.

My read is that this is a good week to know what you agreed to. The shared [robots.txt opt-out for AI training](/news/ai-training-opt-out-robots-txt) gives site owners a cleaner way to say no to several AI companies at once. Meta says robots.txt still works on its side, but the terms themselves are what a regulator is now testing.

## What should you do now?

1. **Do nothing yet.** The current terms still apply.
2. **Read any new terms notice** from Meta before you accept it.
3. **Check your robots.txt** so it reflects what you want crawled.
4. **Follow the [CMA case page](https://www.gov.uk/cma-cases/suspected-abuse-of-a-dominant-position-in-relation-to-metas-commercial-terms)** for updates.
