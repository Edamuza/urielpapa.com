WordCamp Philippines 2026 was a two-day WordPress conference held on August 28 and 29, 2026 at the Iloilo Convention Center in Iloilo City. Day one was Contributor Day and day two was Conference Day, and the event closed with 451 registered attendees from 15 countries.

I supported the event as a micro sponsor, and you can find my name on the [WordCamp Philippines 2026 sponsors page](https://philippines.wordcamp.org/2026/sponsors/ "WordCamp Philippines 2026 sponsors page, including micro sponsors"). WordPress is where I built my first client websites and my team's [five WordPress SEO plugins](/projects/wordpress-seo-plugins "WordPress SEO Plugin Suite case study"). This note is part of my [Technology and Business](/topics/tech-and-business "Technology and Business: posts on how tech affects business") writing.

## What was WordCamp Philippines 2026?

WordCamp Philippines 2026 was the national WordCamp of the Philippine WordPress community, run by volunteer organizers. The [official highlights post](https://philippines.wordcamp.org/2026/wcph2026-highlights/ "WordCamp Philippines 2026 official highlights and numbers") reports 451 registrants, of whom 434 checked in at the venue. The same post lists 15 speakers and 15 sponsors, with participants from 15 countries.

The City Government of Iloilo, MICE Iloilo City and the Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) supported the event. Former Iloilo City Councilor Jay Treñas gave the welcome remarks on behalf of Mayor Raisa Treñas. The event's mascot was Halopuu, a local take on the WordPress Wapuu.

## Why did I sponsor WordCamp Philippines 2026?

WordCamp Philippines 2026 offered company tiers, from Super Admin down to in-kind sponsors, plus a micro sponsor tier for individuals. The micro sponsor tier lets one person put money behind the event in their own name. Six people were listed as micro sponsors, and I was one of them.

WordPress is the platform I have built client websites on since 2023, and the SEO plugins my team built later all ran on it. Sponsoring a community event is a small way to give back to that platform. It also supports the people who build and teach WordPress in the Philippines.

## What happened on Contributor Day?

Contributor Day is the WordCamp day where attendees work on WordPress itself, not just learn about it. At WordCamp Philippines 2026, 322 contributors joined 12 WordPress teams, and more than 260 of them were first-time contributors, according to the official highlights. The [event schedule](https://philippines.wordcamp.org/2026/schedule/ "WordCamp Philippines 2026 schedule") said that no previous experience was required.

Two side activities ran on the same day. WordPress 101 was a hands-on workshop on building websites and online shops, with 39 participants. The Open Source Library, inspired by the Human Library idea, let attendees "borrow" a person for a conversation about open source, and it logged 36 conversations. Sponsors also helped on the floor: [GreenGeeks' recap](https://www.greengeeks.com/blog/?p=53234 "GreenGeeks recap of WordCamp Philippines 2026") describes two of its staff volunteering at the Support table. A first-timer ratio that high means Contributor Day worked as an on-ramp, not a meeting of insiders.

## What did the keynote say about AI and the open web?

The keynote came from Atty. Jocelle Batapa-Sigue, former DICT Undersecretary for ICT Industry Development. Her talk was titled "From the Open Web to the Age of AI: Building what comes next, responsibly," according to [her own write-up of the keynote](https://jocellebatapasigue.com/2026/09/01/batapa-sigue-brings-ai-open-source-and-digital-governance-into-focus-at-wordcamp-philippines-2026/ "Jocelle Batapa-Sigue's write-up of her WordCamp Philippines 2026 keynote"). Her main point was that AI adds a new layer between people and information, so a website is no longer always where the reader ends up.

She proposed five questions to ask of any AI system:

1. Where did the data come from?
2. Who could be harmed?
3. Who benefits?
4. Can the outputs be explained?
5. Who is accountable?

She tied these to habits the WordPress community already has, such as transparency, peer review, interoperability and documentation. She also cited W3Techs data showing WordPress on 40.7% of all websites as of August 31, 2026. The keynote framed governance as part of building technology, not a step added at the end.

## What does this mean for business owners with a WordPress site?

The keynote's point about AI as a middle layer is the biggest takeaway for a business site. A customer may now meet your content inside an AI answer before they ever load your page. That makes clear, well-structured, quotable content as important as ranking, which is the idea behind [GEO and AEO](/blog/geo-and-aeo-fundamentals "GEO and AEO fundamentals: getting cited by AI search").

The SEO talks point the same way. Local SEO, quick wins in AI search and site speed are all things a small business can act on without a rebuild. If you want tools for this, my list of [open-source SEO and GEO tools on GitHub](/blog/open-source-seo-geo-tools-github "The best open-source SEO and GEO tools on GitHub for AI search") is a good starting point.

The five governance questions also work as a checklist before adding an AI plugin or chatbot to a WordPress site. Knowing where the data goes and who is accountable for the answers is cheaper before launch than after a complaint.

## Who should go to the next WordCamp Philippines?

WordCamp Philippines suits WordPress developers, freelancers, agency owners and marketers who want to meet the local community in person. First-time contributors are clearly welcome, given the 260-plus first-timers this year. Business owners who run their site on WordPress will also find the SEO and AI talks useful without needing to code.
